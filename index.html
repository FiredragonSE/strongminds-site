The work

What companies actually need.

Intelligence

Making sense of a market before you spend on it.

Who buys, who decides, and who already sits in the channel. A written brief you can take into a board or a bid meeting, with gaps marked as gaps.

Entry and bid

Whether a country or a programme is worth going after.

Can you enter, and how. Who you would partner with, how buying works, and what a credible bid looks like. If the honest answer is to wait, the memo says so.

Market access

The first meetings, once the route is clear.

A short list of partners, the first conversations, and someone in the room who has done the work. We work from Stockholm. We do not set up an office for you.

Who it is for

Usually, the product is not the problem.

The call tends to come from a commercial lead, a founder who still sells, or someone running a bid. The company has a technology or defence offer. What is stuck is the way into a country, a buyer, or a programme.

  • A technology company that wants the Nordics or one other European country, and does not need another generic study.
  • A defence or dual-use company with a programme in mind, that needs to know how to approach it.
  • An industrial technology firm whose next customer sits inside an institution.

Better to move on a country or a programme you can name than to commission another landscape nobody has to act on.

How it starts

A call, then a fee, then the work.

  1. We talk. The company, the country or the programme, and what you need to decide.
  2. We send a scope. A fixed fee, what you will get, and what sits outside it.
  3. You get something you can use. If the honest answer is not this market yet, you will have that in writing.
