The workFixed fee, agreed in writing

Most of this starts with one country, or one programme.

Technology and defence companies rarely call for strategy in the abstract. They call because a board wants an answer, a bid is coming, or a country has been discussed for a year and nothing has moved. The fee is fixed before the work starts. What comes back should be something you can brief from.

01

Market intelligence

The work before a bid, a country entry, or a difficult board conversation. We read the market the way a buyer would have to: who purchases, who influences, who already sits in the channel, and which political or regulatory facts change the sale.

  • Market, industry and country work, including what you only learn by asking.
  • Competitors and channels, and where you sit among them.
  • Political and stakeholder maps, when the buyer is a public body.
  • Partner or acquisition names, if that is the question.
What you getA written brief. The people are named. Evidence and judgement are kept apart.
02

Feasibility and entry

One country. Can you go in, and how. That covers the partner you would need, whether the location makes sense, what regulation and procurement will do to the plan, and a sensible order for the first ninety days. Waiting is a legitimate answer.

  • Entry work for a named country, usually in Europe or the Nordics.
  • A new market or a new product, tied to something you already sell.
  • The risks that would stop you, set apart from the ones you can live with.
What you getAn entry memo, a sequence, and a few partner options worth calling.
03

Bid and programme strategy

One programme, named. Who writes the requirement, who influences it, what a credible bid looks like from their side, and whether you go direct, with a partner, or sit this round out. This is often the first piece of work for a defence company.

  • How the programme runs, and the path the buyer walks.
  • Whether a partner or a subcontract is the sensible shape.
  • A way of describing the offer that a specifier can repeat.
What you getA path toward the bid, including what to do before the tender opens.
04

Market access

This comes after the brief, and only if the route looks real. A short partner list, the first conversations, and a note of what was said. We do this from Stockholm, across Europe and the Nordics. We will not pretend to be your local office.

  • The partners and buyers the earlier work has already given a reason to meet.
  • Partnership talks with a point to them.
  • Your own commercial lead still owning the account when we step back.
What you getMeetings that were worth having, and a note of what was said.
05

Stakeholder narrative

Some entries fail on the story, not the product. When the buyer is a ministry, a programme office or a board, you need a way of explaining the offer that those people can hold. Communication and public affairs sit here, attached to an entry or a bid. We do not take them on as a branding exercise.

  • Language a specifier, a ministry or a board can use.
  • An investor story only where it serves that buyer. We do not place capital.
What you getOne account of the company, lined up with the intelligence.
Outside the work

A few things we will turn down.

  • Fundraising, placing a fund, or writing an investor story with no operating question under it.
  • Coaching a founder, or writing a growth plan for a company that has not chosen a country or a programme.
  • Becoming your business-development department. This is a one-person practice.
  • A question we cannot actually read. If it needs a specialist we are not, we say so before there is a fee.
